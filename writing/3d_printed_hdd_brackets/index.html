3D printed HDD brackets for p6 series HP desktops

Written by: Matthew

Originally posted: 22nd of June, 2026
Last updated: 22nd of June, 2026

Problem

This is my HP p6-2304a desktop computer. It's a turd when it comes to performance, however it is still technically my "main" desktop computer as of writing [1].

A year or so ago, I was experimenting with Debian on a different computer and it came to a point where I wanted to install it on this computer since I enjoyed the Linux experience so much. There was a problem though. I already had Windows installed on its single hard drive. I didn't want to deal with the hassle of dual booting, so I looked around the house and found a spare hard drive lying around, so no problems right? Just put it in the computer and then I could continue with the Linux install right?

As it turns out, HP and their brilliant prebuilt case designers in 2012 decided to only give you one HDD mount in this entire chassis. The only other expansion bays in this case was in the form of a 5.25 inch drive bay at the front, which was already populated with a DVD drive that I didn't want to remove. With no way to properly mount my hard drive into this case, I was tempted to give up on my Linux dream and to just continue using the other computer until...

An idea occurred to me

When inspecting the case, I noticed that there was a ton of empty space behind the hard drive. So an idea came to my mind, what if I made a custom bracket which would be designed to fit into this space?

I soon decided that the only way to get my Linux fix on this computer was to design and 3D print a bracket which would allow me to fit more hard drives into this case.

Design process

I first started off with a ruler, pencil and a scrap piece of paper. It was a lot of trial and error, there were many times I rubbed out my drawings. I didn't look at any guides or any references online for other HDD brackets that people have made, I instead drew what felt right to me and could potentially fit in the case. Originally, I planned for there to be only two hard drives slots but after some quick measurements, I realised that I could actually fit three in which I incorporated into the design. My final sketches are pictured below.

Next was to translate my sketches into a 3D CAD software. I decided to use the web version of Sketchup. Yes, I know that Sketchup is not the best when it comes to such CAD, but it was what I was comfortable with at the time. Thankfully, the design was very simple and I had it done an hour or so later.

3D printing

A technology teacher I knew at my school very generously donated his own time into helping me print this for him [2]. I had never 3D printed something before, so the process was me pretty much emailing him the files and then a few days later I got the print. Maybe one day I can figure out how to work one of these things.

I was pretty sure that I had messed up some measurements during my first attempt so to not waste filament, I only sent him the right bracket to print. Sure enough, when I got the print back for a test fit...

The holes for where you would screw the hard drives to the side of the bracket did not line up. As well as that, the bracket was also too tall to fit inside the metal chassis to align with the screw holes. To fix that, I would need to shave off the bottom of the bracket by a few millimeters.

No worries though, a few quick measurements and edits to the model to fix those problems up and I was back on track. During my V2 revision, I also added in some supports for where the hard drives could rest upon while you were screwing them in the bracket, to make the installation process easier.

I excitedly received the print for V2 and sure enough, everything fit perfectly in the case [3]! I can now securely put three 3.5 inch hard drives into my HP computer now. Mission accomplished.

Bonus: power supply modding

This is a pretty major oversight on my end, but during the entire process of making the bracket, I didn't check if the OEM HP 300 watt power supply could actually support all three hard drives as well as a DVD drive. Thankfully, there were exactly four SATA power connectors, however one of them was too short to connect to one of the hard drives located at the bottom. It seemed that HP designed these two SATA power connectors to only support the expansion bays at the front which were why they were so short, however if you wanted to use one of them for any longer runs inside the computer then they would be too short.

Now any sane person in the situation would just buy a SATA extension cable and just be done with it. Not me though. Instead, I realised that I could use an old, previously blown up power supply found in the closet, cut one of its SATA power connectors and then solder it to the HP power supply. So this is what I ended up doing.

As it turns out, this whole process was much easier than I first thought. I realised that you can actually take apart the SATA power connectors on the HP power supply, solder in the longer wires, and then put back the OEM SATA power connector back on your newly spliced wires. This gave the final appearance of the mod a surprisingly professional look, minus the electrical tape of course.

If you are going to do this mod, I would recommend for you to make sure you wire up the SATA power connector correctly. Draw a pinout if necessary as you can seriously damage hardware if you mess this up.

The mod did technically work in the end, however there is a problem. If you wiggle the connector around near the SATA connector, the drive will lose power. This is probably because the wires that I used from that old crappy power supply are a thinner gauge as compared to the HP power supply wires, hence the clamping mechanism which terminates the cables into the connector doesn't work as well.

Side tangent, when I was tinkering around with the power supply I found out that you can actually manually switch this power supply's input voltage between 110/120v to 230/240v just by peeling off the sticker at the back and flicking the switch. How neat.

What I would do differently next time

The main problem with this current setup is dealing with the amount of heat generated by three closely packed hard drives. This problem is further exacerbated by the fact that the case was never designed to support three hard drives (what a surprise right?) and no front air intake. As a result, I saw max temperatures on one of the hard drives reach 45 degrees celsius. Definitely not ideal, especially if you care about hard drive health.

If you are planning to seriously use this bracket, I would recommend for you to either attach a small fan to blow air in between the hard drives from the top or to just install two hard drives instead of three. That would mean one hard drive at the front and one all the way at the back. If I were to make potential improvements to this print (a version 3 you might say), then I would add some breather holes on the sides of the brackets to allow air to escape from the side more effectively.
