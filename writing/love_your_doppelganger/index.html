Love your doppelganger

Written by: Matthew

Originally posted: 6th of November, 2026
Last updated: 6th of November, 2026

Or why you should love everyone...

As you age, the total number of people you talk to grows as a more or less linear function. [1]

You might dismiss this as nothing to take note of, but it comes with unexpected consequences.

At a certain number of people within a crowd of people, you will get to a magical point where you can guarantee that you will find other people that share the same hobbies and interests as yourself. There is actually a tangentially related mathematical proof of this observation known as the Birthday Problem, which shows there is a more than 50% chance of two people sharing a birthday in a randomly chosen group of 23. [2]

Another observation with human socialisation is that we can't help but to prioritize interactions with people who we see ourselves in. The people that we share the same or very similar hobbies and interests as us. This also means we tend to avoid and cull any people who have qualities that we deem unattractive.

This is because when we know about a topic really well, we have a better idea of how our interactions will play out with that person. This is why shared interests are often one of the best conversation starters you can hope for. It also explains how more friendships are formed when everyone there has similar interests like in person conventions.

Of course I generally believe this is a positive thing, however this can result in biases and echo chambers which could be damaging to our long term life goals. While it can be very tempting to eat fast food every day, we can all agree it is at the detriment to our long term health.

This is where our doppelgangers come into play. We tend to forget that our interactions with our doppelgangers are as equally important to keeping us sane. The doppelganger's gift is that they give you an excuse to continually evaluate who you are and what you stand for, which can be easy to forget in our daily lives. [3]

When you engage with your doppelganger, whether that be through a direct one on one conversation or even just observing them from the sidelines, it's an autonomic response from the brain to continually ask the question "Do I see myself in my doppelganger?" If you answer yes, then there comes that cognitive dissonance which is very helpful when you are working on being a better person each day.

If you interact with your doppelganger enough, strange things might happen. Your sense of self becomes more clear, like a squeegee on a window with thick condensation. You will find your true boundaries on your political views and physical body, whether you like it or not.

For example, say that you believe that you should not litter in public places. You then observe from across the hallway that your doppelganger smushes biscuits in the carpet with their feet. Doesn't this interaction strengthen your inner belief that it is not okay to make a mess on public property?

Say that you believe that beggars can't be choosers, and from eaves dropping, you find out that your doppelganger is a beggar who chooses. Doesn’t this information strengthen the neurons, reinforcing the idea in your mind that beggars can't be choosers?

If you notice that your doppelganger is clearly affecting the mob mentality of their less than stellar peers, won’t you think to yourself that in the future you will need to be careful about when you should associate with a potentially unhealthy group of people?

If we understand that our doppelgangers are essential to living our best lives, then this unfortunately implies that we also need to look after them.

This is obviously not something that most people want to or are able to in the first place. Already, many people are struggling with taking care of their friends and family. I get it, if you are in the water already, then why should you bother to deal with people that you don't even want to associate with?

So let's compromise instead, maybe you don't need to talk to your doppelganger everyday, once a week or even once a month. Maybe once a year would be a good start. So do this, put yourself out there once a year and ensure that when you do, you make it as productive as possible.

Just one last thing though, remember to be nice to them! Your doppelganger most likely didn't choose to be in the situation that they found themselves in. Context and upbringing are very large, yet underrepresented areas when analyzing why they are like that.

I feel there is a genuine positive impact when engaging in these types of conversations and when complemented alongside your daily interactions with friends and family, then I think you will be better off because of them.
